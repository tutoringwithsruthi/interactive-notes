Structures of Expressions

Naming the parts of an expression, and saying what each one means in the situation it came from.

MapWhat this unit covers

This is a short unit that runs alongside everything else. It is about being precise: knowing what a term is, what a factor is, and being able to point at any piece of an expression and say what it does.

What Math 1 leaves out

Polynomial operations are limited. You add and subtract quadratics, and you add, subtract, and multiply linear expressions. Multiplying two quadratics is Math 2.

Simplifying square roots into radical form is not expected. $\sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$ is a Math 2 move. Here you only take roots that come out cleanly.

L1The vocabulary

These words get used loosely in conversation and precisely on assessments. The difference between a term and a factor is what separates adding from multiplying.

Term
a piece separated by $+$ or $-$. In $3x^2 + 5x - 7$ the terms are $3x^2$, $5x$, and $-7$.
Factor
a piece being multiplied. In $3x^2$ the factors are 3, $x$, and $x$. In $(x+2)(x-5)$ the factors are the two brackets.
Coefficient
the number multiplying a variable. In $5x$ it is 5; in $-x$ it is $-1$.
Constant
a term with no variable, like the $-7$ above.
Base and exponent
in $2^t$ the base is 2 and the exponent is $t$. In $x^3$ the base is $x$.
Click a part name to highlight it
Terms are added, factors are multiplied $3x + 5$ has two terms. $3(x + 5)$ has two factors, one of which is a bracket containing two terms. They look similar and behave completely differently — you can distribute across the second and not the first.

L2What each part means

Naming a part earns half the mark. Saying what it represents earns the rest, and it must be said in the units of the situation.

A helium balloon: its height in feet is $5 + 6.3s$, where $s$ is seconds since release.
Two terms: $5$ and $6.3s$.
The constant 5: the height in feet at the moment of release — how high the person was holding it.
The coefficient 6.3: the rate of rise, in feet per second.
Positive, so it climbs. A negative coefficient would mean it was sinking.
A basketball: its height in metres is $-4.9t^2 + 17t + 0.6$, with $t$ in seconds.
Constant 0.6: release height — the ball left the hands 0.6 m above the ground.
Coefficient 17: the initial upward speed, in metres per second.
Coefficient $-4.9$: the pull of gravity. Negative, which is why the ball comes back down and why the parabola opens downward.
A car's value: $35000(0.87)^t$, with $t$ in years since purchase.
Factor 35000: the purchase price, since $(0.87)^0 = 1$.
Base 0.87: the decay factor — the car keeps 87% of its value each year, so it loses 13%.
Exponent $t$: the number of times that factor has been applied.
Careful: 0.87 is the factor, not the rate. The rate is 13%.
Interpret every part of $1200(1.04)^x$, the balance of a savings account $x$ years after opening.
1200 is the initial deposit in dollars — the balance when $x = 0$, since $(1.04)^0 = 1$.
1.04 is the growth factor: the balance is multiplied by 1.04 each year.
The rate is $1.04 - 1 = 0.04$, an interest rate of 4% per year.
$x$ is the number of years, and also the number of times interest has been applied.
The whole expression is a product of two factors: 1200 and $(1.04)^x$. It has one term, not two.

L3Adding and subtracting polynomials

NC frames this as an analogy worth taking seriously: polynomial arithmetic works like integer arithmetic. You combine like with like, exactly as you add tens to tens and ones to ones.

Like terms have the same variable raised to the same power. $3x^2$ and $8x^2$ are like terms. $3x^2$ and $3x$ are not, and can never be combined.

Subtract: $(4x + 3) - (2x + 1)$
Distribute the minus across everything in the bracket: $4x + 3 - 2x - 1$
Combine like terms: $2x + 2$
The step everyone drops: the minus applies to both terms. Writing $4x + 3 - 2x + 1$ is the single most common error in this unit.
Simplify: $(x^2 + 5x - 9) + 2x(4x - 3)$
Distribute first: $2x(4x-3) = 8x^2 - 6x$
Now add: $x^2 + 5x - 9 + 8x^2 - 6x$
Group like terms: $(x^2 + 8x^2) + (5x - 6x) - 9$
Result: $9x^2 - x - 9$
Check any simplification by comparing values

Write two equivalent expressions for the area of a circle whose radius is $5x - 2$ km.
Factored: $A = \pi(5x - 2)^2$
Expanded: $(5x-2)^2 = 25x^2 - 20x + 4$, so $A = 25\pi x^2 - 20\pi x + 4\pi$

Both describe the same area. The factored form makes the radius visible; the expanded form makes the quadratic structure visible. Which one is "better" depends entirely on what you want to read off it — the same argument as factored versus standard form in Unit 6.

L4Rewriting with integer exponents

The exponent rules from 8th grade now apply to expressions with several variables. Nothing about the rules changes — there is just more bookkeeping.

$a^m \cdot a^n = a^{m+n}$
same base multiplied, add exponents
$\dfrac{a^m}{a^n} = a^{m-n}$
same base divided, subtract exponents
$(a^m)^n = a^{mn}$
power of a power, multiply exponents
$(ab)^m = a^m b^m$
a power distributes over a product — including over the coefficient
$a^{-n} = \dfrac{1}{a^n}$
negative exponent means reciprocal
Simplify $(8x^{-4}y^3)(-2x^5y^{-6})^2$
Square the second bracket first — the exponent hits every factor inside, coefficient included: $(-2)^2 = 4$, $(x^5)^2 = x^{10}$, $(y^{-6})^2 = y^{-12}$.
So it becomes $(8x^{-4}y^3)(4x^{10}y^{-12})$
Multiply: coefficients $8 \cdot 4 = 32$;  $x^{-4} \cdot x^{10} = x^6$;  $y^3 \cdot y^{-12} = y^{-9}$
Result: $32x^6y^{-9}$, or with no negative exponents, $\dfrac{32x^6}{y^9}$
Note the squared negative: $(-2)^2 = 4$, positive. Forgetting to square the sign is a frequent slip.
Simplify $\dfrac{(3m^2p^{-2}q)^3}{9m^{-3}q^3}$
Cube the top: $3^3 = 27$, $(m^2)^3 = m^6$, $(p^{-2})^3 = p^{-6}$, $q^3$. So the numerator is $27m^6p^{-6}q^3$.
Divide: $\dfrac{27}{9} = 3$;  $m^{6-(-3)} = m^9$;  $q^{3-3} = q^0 = 1$
Result: $3m^9p^{-6}$, or $\dfrac{3m^9}{p^6}$
The $q$ vanished because $q^0 = 1$, not because it was cancelled away carelessly.
Exponent drill with variables
Simplify $\sqrt{25m^{14}p^2t^4}$, assuming every variable is positive.
Take the square root of each factor: $\sqrt{25} = 5$, $\sqrt{m^{14}} = m^7$, $\sqrt{p^2} = p$, $\sqrt{t^4} = t^2$.
Result: $5m^7pt^2$.
Every exponent inside was even and 25 was a perfect square, which is why this comes out cleanly. Math 1 only asks for roots that do — anything needing $\sqrt{50} = 5\sqrt{2}$ is Math 2.

✓Quick review

Term vs factor
terms are separated by $+$ and $-$; factors are multiplied together
Interpreting
name the part, then say what it means in the units of the situation
Factor vs rate
a base of 0.87 is a factor; the rate is a 13% decrease
Subtracting a bracket
the minus applies to every term inside, not just the first
Like terms
same variable, same power; $x^2$ and $x$ never combine
Power of a product
$(-2x^5)^2 = 4x^{10}$ — the exponent hits the coefficient and its sign too
$a^0 = 1$
why variables disappear when exponents subtract to zero

If you remember one thing: two expressions are equivalent only if they agree for every value. Substituting one number is a fast way to catch an error, though agreeing at one point does not by itself prove equivalence.
